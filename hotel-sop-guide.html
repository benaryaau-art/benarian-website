01 · THE FOUNDATION

An SOP is a controlled operating promise.

A hotel SOP translates a policy or service standard into repeatable action. It explains who performs the task, when it begins, what sequence is required, which controls protect the guest and the business, and what evidence proves completion.

Design principle: write for the employee performing the task during a busy shift. Every instruction should be observable, teachable and auditable.

What a good SOP prevents

  • Inconsistent guest experiences
  • Missed handovers and unclear ownership
  • Safety, security and compliance failures
  • Revenue leakage, waste and rework
  • Knowledge loss when employees leave

What a good SOP creates

  • Clear authority and escalation
  • Consistent service at every shift
  • Faster onboarding and cross-training
  • Measurable quality and accountability
  • A reliable base for improvement

02 · DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE

Use the right document for the right level.

Policy

States the organisation’s rule and intent: what must always be true and who holds final accountability.

Standard

Defines the required result, timing or quality level, such as room-ready time or response time.

Standard Operating Procedure

Explains the repeatable sequence, roles, controls, exceptions and records required to deliver the standard.

Work instruction & checklist

Provides task-level detail and the evidence used during a shift. Keep it short, visual and usable at the work point.

03 · TEN-STEP METHOD

Design the procedure with the people who use it.

  1. Define the guest, safety or commercial outcome.
  2. Name one accountable SOP owner.
  3. Observe the task on different shifts.
  4. Map triggers, steps, decisions and handovers.
  5. Identify hazards, controls and approvals.
  6. Write active instructions using one action per step.
  7. Add exceptions and escalation points.
  8. Pilot with new and experienced employees.
  9. Train, observe and certify competence.
  10. Audit evidence and improve the SOP.

06 · TRAINING

Training turns a document into behaviour.

Tell → Show → Do → Check

Explain the purpose and risks, demonstrate the task at normal pace, let the employee perform it, then observe against the checklist. A signature confirms attendance; observed competence confirms capability.

Training evidence

Maintain a role-based training matrix with required SOPs, trainer, completion date, assessment result, refresher date and any coaching action.

Certification rule: employees should not perform a controlled task alone until a designated trainer has observed the complete procedure and signed the competence record.

07 · CONTROL & AUDIT

Keep one approved version at the point of use.

Version control

  • Unique ID and named owner
  • Approval before issue
  • Revision history and effective date
  • Archived superseded copies
  • Scheduled and event-triggered review

Audit method

  • Observe the work, not only the paperwork
  • Sample records across shifts
  • Ask employees to explain escalation
  • Grade critical and non-critical gaps
  • Assign action, owner and due date

08 · GOVERNANCE

Measure whether the SOP produces the intended result.

Operational measures

Completion rate, inspection pass rate, repeat errors, guest complaints, response time, rework, waste, incidents and audit closure time.

Review triggers

New equipment or system, legal or brand change, serious incident, recurring complaint, process redesign, audit failure or annual review date.

SOP effectiveness = compliance evidence + operating result + employee competence
